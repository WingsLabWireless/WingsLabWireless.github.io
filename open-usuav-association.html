Open USUAV Association

Advancing open, research-grade UAV platforms for U.S. leadership, university-scale experimentation, and hands-on education.

Open Source Research-Grade UAVs Education & Workforce Testbed Integration

The Open USUAV Association is a community-driven organization dedicated to maintaining and advancing the WINGS Open USUAV Ecosystem—a family of open, software-defined, AI-enabled UAV platforms designed for research, education, and real-world deployment.

The name USUAV reflects our dual mission:

  • to strengthen the United States’ leadership in uncrewed aerial systems (UAS) development and applications; and
  • to provide a university-scale, fully open-source UAV platform that can be adopted, replicated, and extended by academic, industrial, and government partners.
Relationship to WINGS Aerial Systems: The Open USUAV Association focuses on the community, standards, and open-source ecosystem. WINGS Aerial Systems, a startup founded by Dr.~Zhangyu Guan, provides manufacturing, integration, and professional support for organizations that need turnkey hardware based on the open designs.

1.2) Vision & Mission

Vision

A world where any university, lab, or agency can deploy safe, research-grade UAV platforms using open designs, interoperable standards, and a vibrant community of contributors.

Mission

The Open USUAV Association exists to:

  • steward the open hardware, software, and documentation of the WINGS Open USUAV Ecosystem;
  • enable reproducible, university-scale UAV experimentation and education nationwide;
  • strengthen the domestic open-source UAV supply chain and workforce pipeline;
  • coordinate interoperability across testbeds (e.g., UMore Park, SOAR, UnionLabs);
  • promote safe, standards-based UAV deployment in research, education, and field applications.

2) WINGS Open USUAV Ecosystem

The Association’s primary technical focus is the WINGS Open USUAV Ecosystem, which includes:

  • Open hardware designs for multiple UAV classes (lightweight to heavy-lift), including frames, power systems, carrier boards, payload mounts, and environmental protection features.
  • Software-defined flight and networking stack built on COTS autopilots (e.g., Cube flight controllers), PX4/ArduPilot, MAVLink, multi-modal wireless (RC, Wi-Fi, 4G/5G, SDR), and AI-enabled autonomy layers.
  • Tuning and safety methodology, including open-source parameter sets, tuning procedures, and safety checklists adapted to each WINGS airframe.
  • Integration with testbeds and ranges such as UMore Park (UMN) and SOAR (UB).
  • Educational and training resources used in conjunction with UnionLabs and UAV courses, camps, and workshops.

2.2) Core Activities

Open-Source Stewardship

The Association maintains GitHub repositories for hardware designs, software stacks, tuning files, and documentation, ensuring high-quality releases and long-term sustainability.

Technical Standards

We define and update standards for airframes, power-distribution, connectors, wiring, payload interfaces, autonomy modules, and safety practices to support reproducible deployments.

Tutorials & Training

The Association curates tutorials, step-by-step guides, and example experiments to help new users adopt the ecosystem and to support workforce development and education.

Community Events

We coordinate summits, workshops, and student-focused events to share best practices, research results, and lessons learned from field deployments across the country.

Testbed Interoperability

By aligning control interfaces and APIs, the Association ensures that Open USUAV platforms can be deployed on testbeds such as SOAR, and UMore Park without modifying existing infrastructure.

Domestic Innovation & Supply Chain

Working with partners like WINGS Aerial Systems, we support a healthy domestic ecosystem for manufacturing, supporting, and deploying open, research-grade UAV platforms.

3) Community & Governance

3.1) Governance Model

The Open USUAV Association follows a lightweight, transparent governance structure inspired by successful open-source projects:

  • Steering Committee: oversees the roadmap, standards, and major releases. Members include representatives from universities, testbeds, industry, and key maintainers.
  • Technical Working Groups: focus on hardware, software, wireless networking, safety/autonomy, and education.
  • Community Contributors: students, researchers, engineers, and educators who submit issues, pull requests, documentation, and example experiments.

Decisions are recorded publicly, and discussions are held via open online channels to ensure community visibility and participation.

3.2) Membership

Membership in the Open USUAV Association is open to:

  • universities and research labs,
  • government agencies and testbed operators,
  • industry partners, startups, and integrators,
  • individual students, educators, and hobbyists.

Members may participate in working groups, propose improvements, and help shape the technical and educational roadmap.

4) Resources

4.1) Technical Resources

The Association maintains a set of open resources (hosted on public repositories and documentation sites), including:

  • hardware CAD models, schematics, PCB layouts, and bills of materials;
  • firmware and software repositories for flight control, networking, and autonomy;
  • flight-controller tuning files and safety checklists for each WINGS airframe;
  • datasets and reference experiments for research and coursework.

4.2) Events & Training

The Open USUAV Association plans to organize:

  • online tutorials and webinars on building, tuning, and flying USUAV platforms;
  • hands-on workshops at partner testbeds and flight ranges;
  • student competitions, hackathons, and capstone project themes;
  • an annual Open USUAV Summit to share research and best practices.

5) Get Involved

5.1) How to Join

There are several ways to participate in the Open USUAV Association:

  • Technical contributions: submit pull requests, bug reports, and documentation improvements to the WINGS Open USUAV repositories.
  • Deployment sites: adopt and deploy USUAV platforms at your flight range, lab, or testbed and share lessons learned with the community.
  • Education & outreach: integrate USUAV platforms into courses, workshops, and camps and contribute teaching materials back to the ecosystem.
  • Partnerships: collaborate on joint experiments, proposals, and workforce development initiatives.

5.2) Contact

For collaboration inquiries, membership questions, or technical discussions, please contact the Open USUAV Association via the primary coordination team (e.g., WINGS Lab at the University of Minnesota).
